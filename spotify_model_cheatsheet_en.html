Origin
Created by Spotify in 2012 by Henrik Kniberg and Anders Ivarsson. Published as a whitepaper that went viral in the agile world. It is not a framework — it is a set of principles that worked for Spotify at a specific moment in time.
Problem it solves
Scaling agility without bureaucracy. How to maintain startup speed and innovation while the company grows to hundreds of engineers across multiple products.
⚠️ Important warning
Spotify itself no longer uses the model as originally described. Adapt to your context — copying it literally without understanding the principles is the most common mistake.

The 4 structures of the Spotify model
🟦
Squad
Autonomous multidisciplinary team
"Mini-startup" inside the company. Owns a mission and end-to-end of a product or feature.
6–12 people (pizza scale)
Multidiscipliner: dev, UX, QA, data, PO
Autonomy to decide how to achieve their mission
Chooses its own process (Scrum, Kanban, etc)
Own stack — responsible for the full cycle
1 Product Owner per squad
Alignment: Mission · Squad KPIs
🏘
Tribe
Collection of related squads
Group of squads working in related areas and sharing a larger business mission.
Up to ~100 people (Dunbar's Law)
Led by a Tribe Lead (not a micromanager)
Coordination between squads via Chapter and Sync
Shares physical (or virtual) space when possible
Tribes above 100 people should be split
Tribe OKRs cascade from company strategy
Alignment: Tribe OKRs
📐
Chapter
Disciplina dentro da tribe
Community of people with the same expertise within a tribe. Ensures technical standards and career growth.
Crosses squads within the same tribe
Led by the Chapter Lead (also a squad member)
Chapter Lead is the career manager of members
Defines practices, tools and standards of the discipline
Regular meetings to share best practices
E.g.: Backend Chapter, UX Research Chapter
Alignment: craft and career
🌐
Guild
Comunidade de interest ampla
Informal community of practice that crosses entire tribes. Voluntary participation, no formal hierarchy.
Crosses multiple tribes and even business teams
No Chapter Lead — self-organized
Focus on interest and passion, not on reporting line
Facilitates knowledge dissemination at scale
Slack channels, internal meetups, technical newsletters
E.g.: Accessibility Guild, Machine Learning Guild
Alignment: interest and passion
Key roles — who does what
Product Owner (PO)
SQUAD · 1 per squad
Defines and prioritizes the squad backlog. Represents the customer and business. Owns the "what" — the squad decides the "how". Is part of the squad, not outside it.
Agile Coach / SM
SQUAD · shared
Facilitates the squad's agile process. Removes impediments, protects the team from interruptions. Usually shared across 2–3 squads. Not a manager.
Chapter Lead
CHAPTER · career manager
Leads the technical discipline (frontend, backend, QA). Is the formal manager of chapter members: hiring, feedback, development and promotion. Also a squad member.
Tribe Lead
TRIBE · VP of product
Responsible for the tribe's mission and results. Sets the environment and culture. Facilitates coordination between squads. Does not micromanage squads — removes blockers and creates context.
Autonomy vs. alignment — the central balance of the model
How to ensure alignment
Clear and stable mission over a long period
Company OKRs → tribe → squad
Monthly tribe all-hands with results
Weekly squad sync between tribe POs
How to ensure autonomy
Squad decides its process (Scrum, Kanban)
Squad chooses and maintains its own stack
Deploy without approval from another team
Fail and learn without blame
Regra de ouro: "Be aligned on the why and the what — but autonomous on the how."

Core principles of the Spotify model
🍕
Pizza Rule
A squad should be small enough to be fed with two pizzas. Squads that are too large lose cohesion, speed and autonomy. If it grew too big, split it.
🧭
Mission, not tasks
Leaders define the destination and the why. Squads choose the path. Assigning tasks kills creativity. Assigning missions unlocks innovation. "What" and "why" come from above. "How" comes from the squad.
🔓
Fail fast, learn faster
Squads are encouraged to experiment and fail fast. Controlled failure is cheaper than paralysis. Culture of blameless post-mortems — the system failed, not the person.
🏗
You build it, you run it
The squad that develops is the same one that operates and monitors in production. Eliminates the "blame game" between dev and ops. Generates real accountability for quality and reliability.
🎵
Culture before process
The Spotify model is not a set of ceremonies — it is a cultural philosophy. High-trust teams need less process. Invest in culture first, then in structure.
🔄
Continuous improvement
Each squad retrospects and adapts its own process. The company's model also evolves. There is no final version — there are progressively better versions for the current context.
Common pitfalls — and how to avoid them
⚠️
Copying literally without adapting
"We're implementing the Spotify Model across the entire company this quarter"
Understand the principles, experiment with 1–2 squads and gradually adapt to your context
⚠️
Chapter Lead becomes a full-time manager
Chapter Lead stops being a squad member and becomes a full-time career manager
Chapter Lead must be an active squad member — leadership is part of the role, not all of it
⚠️
Autonomy without alignment
Fully autonomous squads without clear OKRs go in opposite directions and create duplication
Ensure strategic alignment via OKRs and clear missions before releasing autonomy
⚠️
Squads without real end-to-end ownership
A squad that depends on 3 other teams to deploy is not autonomous — it is a silo with a nice name
Invest in platform engineering so squads can deploy and monitor independently
⚠️
Tribes that are too large
A tribe of 200+ people loses cohesion, communication fails and coordination becomes a permanent meeting
Split tribes that exceed ~100 people. Use Dunbar's Law as a size guide
⚠️
Guild vs. Chapter confused
A Guild with hierarchy and goals becomes a Chapter without a clear reporting line — worst of both worlds
Chapter = discipline + career + inside tribe. Guild = interest + voluntary + cross-company
