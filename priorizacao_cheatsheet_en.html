RICE
RICE Score — quantitative feature prioritization
Created by Intercom · compares features on an objective scale, eliminating opinion bias
(R × I × C) ÷ E
fórmula do score
Reach · R
R
Reach
How many people will be impacted by this feature in a defined period (month, quarter)?
ex: 500 usuários/mês
Impact · I
I
Impact per person
What is the impact on each affected user's life? Use your company's standardized scale.
3=massivo 2=alto 1=médio 0.5=baixo 0.25=mínimo
Confidence · C
C
Confidence
How confident are the Reach and Impact estimates? Penalizes guesswork and unvalidated hypotheses.
100%=alta 80%=média 50%=baixa
Effort · E
E
Effort
How many person-months of work does this feature require? Includes design, development and QA.
ex: 2 person-months
Score final
= R×I×C÷E
Higher = higher priority

WSJF
Weighted Shortest Job First — priorização por custo de atraso
Originated in Lean · popularized in SAFe · focuses on the cost of NOT delivering something now
CoD ÷ Tamanho
Cost of Delay ÷ Job Size
Cost of Delay (CoD) = sum of the 3 factors below
BV
Business Value
Direct business value generated. Revenue, customer satisfaction, competitive advantage.
TV
Time Criticality
Delivery urgency. Does value decay with delay? Is there a regulatory or market deadline?
RR
Risk Reduction / Opportunity Enablement
Reduces future risk or enables other deliveries? E.g.: infra, architecture, technical enablers.

Compare items to each other ("Is this 3× more urgent than that?"), not in absolute values.

⭐ Quick Wins — Faça agora
High impact · Low effort
Priority máxima. Entregam valor rápido com pouco custo. São o alvo perfeito para o próximo ciclo.
🏔 Grandes Projetos — Planeje
High impact · High effort
Worth the investment, but requires careful planning. Break into smaller pieces before starting.
🍬 Fill-ins — Se sobrar
Low impact · Low effort
Don't actively prioritize. Do only if there's idle capacity — never instead of Quick Wins.
🚫 Traps — Evite
Low impact · High effort
Cancel or postpone indefinitely. They consume capacity without generating value. Reassess if context changes.
MoSCoW
MoSCoW — scope prioritization by category
Created by Dai Clegg (Oracle, 1994) · widely used in DSDM, Scrum and release negotiation with clients
Must · Should · Could · Won't
4 categorias de escopo
M
Must have
Must have
~60% do esforço
Non-negotiable requirements. Without them, the product cannot be delivered or has no value.
Without this, the product fails
Legal, security or mission-critical
Everything else is secondary
S
Should have
Should have
~20% do esforço
Important but not critical. If time is tight, it can be dropped — but should come in as early as possible.
High priority sem ser vital
Impacts experience, not delivery
Candidate for the next cycle
C
Could have
Could have
~20% do esforço
Desirable if time and resources allow. Improves experience but its absence causes no real pain.
Conscious nice-to-have
First to go under pressure
Backlog for future versions
W
Won't have
Won't have now
0% neste ciclo
Explicitly out of scope for this cycle. Does not mean "never" — means "not now". Reduces expectation conflict.
Avoids implicit scope
Aligns expectations early
Can become Must in next cycle
Example — Release 1.0 of management platform
Must
Login and secure authentication with MFA
Must
Full task CRUD with assignee and deadline
Should

Should

Dashboard with progress chart by project
Won't
Slack and Microsoft Teams integration
Won't
Native mobile app (iOS/Android)
⚠️ Pitfall: If everything becomes Must, MoSCoW loses meaning. Impose limits: Musts cannot exceed 60% of team capacity. Won't is as important as Must — it eliminates scope ambiguity.
