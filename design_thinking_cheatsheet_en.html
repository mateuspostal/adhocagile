Mindset — the 4 pillars of Design Thinking
🧠
Empathize radical
Get out from behind the desk. Observe, listen, and feel the real needs of users — without judgment.
🔀
Diverge before converging
Generate many ideas before judging. Quantity first, quality later. Avoid "yes, but...".
🧪
Prototype to learn
Make something with your hands. A paper prototype that fails early is 100x cheaper than a product that fails late.
🔁
Embrace ambiguity
The best problems have no obvious answer. Being comfortable with uncertainty is a prerequisite for innovation.

POV and HMW — synthesizing the problem
POV
Point of View — problem statement
[Usuário] needs [necessidade] porque [surprising insight]
Exemplo
"Ana, gerente de projetos sobrecarregada, precisa enxergar o status real do time em segundos porque reuniões de 1h consomem seu tempo mais valioso sem gerar decisões."
HMW
How Might We — invitation to ideate
How might we [verbo de ação] [para quem] [de forma que] [benefício]?
Exemplos — cada HMW vira uma sessão de ideação
· "How might we mostrar o status do time para Ana em menos de 5 segundos?"
· "How might we eliminar reuniões de status sem perder visibilidade?"
· "How might we transformar dados do time em decisões automáticas?"
Ideation techniques
TECHNIQUE 01
Brainstorming
Free group ideation session. 4 rules: no judgment, quantity equals quality, build on others' ideas, encourage unusual ideas.
⏱ 20–30 min · 4–8 pessoas · post-its
TECHNIQUE 02
Crazy 8s
Each person folds paper into 8 parts and sketches 8 different ideas in 8 minutes (1 min per sketch). Forces fast individual divergence before the group.
⏱ 8 min · individual · caneta + papel
TECHNIQUE 03
SCAMPER
Substitute · Combine · Adapt · Modify · Put to other uses · Eliminate · Reverse. Apply each verb to the problem.
⏱ 30–45 min · structured adaptation
TECHNIQUE 04
Worst Possible Idea
Deliberately generate the worst possible solutions to the problem. Then invert each bad idea to find valid solutions and unlock blocked creativity.
⏱ 15 min · great for stuck groups
TECHNIQUE 05
Dot Voting
Each participant receives 3–5 sticky dots (or votes) to distribute among preferred ideas. Fast, democratic and visual. Selects ideas with the most potential.
⏱ 5–10 min · silent convergence
TECHNIQUE 06
Impact × Effort Matrix
Map selected ideas into 4 quadrants: Quick Wins (high impact, low effort) · Big projects · Fill-ins · Thankless tasks. Prioritize quick wins.
⏱ 20 min · rational prioritization
Prototype types — from idea to testable
LOW FIDELITY
Paper Prototype
Hand-drawn screens on paper. Fast, cheap, disposable. Allows testing flows without code or design.
→ Use early in ideation
LOW FIDELITY
Storyboard
Sequence of frames illustrating the user journey with the solution. Ideal for validating context and narrative before the interface.
→ Use to validate the full flow
MEDIUM FIDELITY
Wireframe Clicável
Digital prototype without visual style (Figma, Balsamiq). Tests navigation and interaction logic without visual distractions.
→ Use to test usability
HIGH FIDELITY
Mockup / MVP
Prototype with final visuals or minimum viable product. Tests value perception, desirability and technical feasibility with real users.
→ Use to validate desire and value
User testing protocol
01 · PREPARE
Define hypotheses to test. Recruit 5 representative users. Prepare the task script without revealing the solution.
02 · CONTEXTUALIZE
Explain that you're testing the product, not the user. Ask them to think aloud. Don't help or lead their answers.
03 · OBSERVE
Note behaviors, hesitations, and errors. Separate observations from interpretations. One observer per task is ideal.
04 · DEBRIEF
Ask about the overall experience. Explore moments of confusion. Ask "why" more than "what". Don't defend the prototype.
05 · SYNTHESIZE
Group findings by pattern. Separate critical, moderate and minor issues. Define what to iterate before the next cycle. Document learnings.
💡 Nielsen's Rule: 5 users reveal ~85% of usability issues in an interface. You don't need to test with dozens of people to get valuable insights.
