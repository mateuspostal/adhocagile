Pyramid DIKW — Data → Information → Knowledge → Wisdom
Data Data
Raw facts without context. Numbers, records, isolated events. Without interpretation, they have no meaning.
Ex: "482 open tickets" · "temperature = 38°C" · "response time = 820ms"
Information Information
Data with context and relationship. Answers "who, what, when, where". Organizes and structures data.
Ex: "Tickets increased 40% compared to last month due to the v2.0 launch"
Knowledge Knowledge
Information applied with experience. Answers "how". Enables action and decision-making. Can be tacit or explicit.
Ex: "When we launch large features without feature flags, tickets spike. The solution is gradual rollout."
Wisdom Wisdom
Knowledge integrated with judgment and values. Answers "why" and "when not to act". Extremely difficult to codify.
Ex: "Not every support problem should be solved with technology — some require process change."
Types of Knowledge — Tacit vs. Explicit
🧠
Tacit Knowledge
Hard to articulate · lives in people
Based on experience, intuition and practical skill
Not written anywhere — only exists in use
Transferred through observation, mentoring and joint practice
Critical loss when the employee leaves the organization
Examples: how a senior dev detects bugs by "feeling" the code · how a salesperson reads the client · a chef's secret recipe
📄
Explicit Knowledge
Documented · transferable · scalable
Codified in documents, wikis, processes and manuals
Can be stored, searched and replicated easily
Transferred through reading, training and e-learning
Loses value if not maintained and updated regularly
Examples: incident runbooks · ADRs (Architecture Decision Records) · FAQs · sales playbooks
SECI Model — Nonaka & Takeuchi (1995) · knowledge creation spiral
← TO: Tacit Explicit →
FROM: Tacit ↕ Explicit
Socialization
Tacit → Tacit
Sharing direct experiences. Knowledge flows through interaction, not documentation.
🤝 Mentoring · pair programming · gemba · shadowing
"Junior learns from senior through observation"
Externalization
Tacit → Explicit
Articulating tacit knowledge into explicit language. The hardest step — uses metaphors, analogies and models.
📝 Wikis · ADRs · interviews · post-mortems · storytelling
"Senior writes the runbook of what they do intuitively"
Internalization
Explicit → Tacit
Learning by doing. Documented knowledge becomes practical skill through repeated experience.
🎯 Training · simulations · on-the-job learning · katas
"Junior follows the runbook until mastering it from memory"
Combination
Explicit → Explicit
Combining and systematizing explicit knowledge from multiple sources to create new explicit knowledge.
📊 Reports · dashboards · knowledge bases · taxonomies
"Runbooks from different teams become a unified knowledge base"
Knowledge management cycle
01
Identify
Map where critical knowledge exists in the organization: who knows what, what is documented and what only lives in people.
→ Knowledge inventory
›
02
Capture
Record and externalize tacit knowledge: interviews, post-mortems, wikis, lessons learned and ADRs.
→ Structured documentation
›
03
Organize
Structure and categorize knowledge into taxonomies, tags and searchable bases. Ensure it is findable.
→ Knowledge base
›
04
Share
Distribute knowledge through the right channels: CoPs, onboarding, internal newsletters, demos and Q&A forums.
→ Sharing culture
›
05
Apply
Use knowledge to solve real problems. Application generates new insights and mistakes that feed back into the cycle.
→ Better decisions
›
06
Update
Continuously review, deprecate and improve what was documented. Outdated knowledge is more dangerous than the absence of it.
→ Knowledge vivo
↺ The cycle is continuous — applying generates new knowledge that feeds back into Capture
KM Strategies — codification vs. personalization
💾
Codification Strategy
Focus on documenting and systematizing knowledge for reuse at scale. The repository is the central asset. Works well when work is repeatable.
Best for: support, high-volume consulting, compliance
Wikis Runbooks FAQs LMS
🤝
Personalization Strategy
Focus on connecting people who hold the knowledge. The relationship is the central asset. Ideal for highly specialized and contextual work.
Best for: strategy, R&D, innovation, complex consulting
Mentoria CoP Directories Office hours
🔄
Hybrid Strategy
Combines documentation with expert networks. The document points to the right person. Recommended for most agile organizations.
Best for: product teams, squads, scale-ups

Communities of Practice (CoP) — Wenger (1998)
How to structure a CoP
Define the domain: what problem does this CoP solve?
Name a facilitator (steward) with dedicated time
Regular cadence: bi-weekly meeting of 60–90 min
Async channel (Slack, Teams) for daily exchange
Measure value: usage stories, problems solved
🏗
Engineering CoP
Architecture, coding best practices, ADRs, security and cross-cutting technical decisions.
🎨
Design CoP
Design system, user research, UX criteria and accessibility standards.
📦
Product CoP
Product strategy, prioritization frameworks, discovery and success metrics.
⚙️
Agile/DevOps CoP
Agile rituals, CI/CD, retrospectives, flow metrics and continuous improvement.
Roles in a CoP
Steward — facilitates, manages the agenda and CoP vitality ·
Core members — actively participate and contribute content ·
Active members — participate when relevant ·
Peripheral members — observe and consume content ·
KM Barriers vs. Enablers
🚧 Barriers — what blocks KM
Silo culture — "knowledge is power, I won't share"
Perceived lack of time — "I have no time to document"
Outdated documentation nobody trusts
Fragmented tools — knowledge scattered across 8 places
No recognition for those who contribute
Leadership that does not model sharing behavior
Weak onboarding that fails to transfer institutional knowledge
✅ Enablers — what makes KM work
Psychological safety — people share mistakes without fear
Protected time for documentation and CoPs in the schedule
Single well-maintained source of truth (wiki, Notion, Confluence)
Explicit recognition for those who teach and document
Knowledge-generating rituals: retrospectives, post-mortems
Structured onboarding with buddy and reading track
Leaders who teach, ask questions and model vulnerability

Level 1 — ChaoticKnowledge exists only in people. No process. High hero dependency. Total loss when someone leaves.
Level 2 — ReactiveDocumentation happens after crises. Wikis exist but are outdated. KM is seen as cost, not investment.
Level 3 — DefinedBasic processes exist: onboarding, post-mortems, ADRs. CoPs emerging. Knowledge base in use.
Level 4 — ManagedKM measured (coverage, search, usage). Active CoPs with stewards. Knowledge feeds strategic decisions.
Level 5 — OptimizedContinuous organizational learning. KM integrated into workflow. Knowledge as measurable competitive advantage.
