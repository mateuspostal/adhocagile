What is ITIL 4
Best practice framework for IT Service Management (ITSM). Version 4 (2019) integrates Agile, DevOps and Lean into the classic model, with a focus on value co-creation.
Core concept
A service is a means of co-creating value by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without them having to manage specific costs and risks.
What's new in ITIL 4 vs v3
Moved from a 5-phase lifecycle to the Service Value System (SVS). Integrated Agile, DevOps and Lean. Made practices (formerly "processes") more flexible and contextual.

7 Guiding principles — applicable to any context
01
Focus on value
Everything the organization does must, directly or indirectly, create value for stakeholders. Value is always defined by the service consumer.
"What does this deliver to the consumer?"
02
Start where you are
Don't start from scratch. Assess the current state, reuse what already works and improve incrementally. Preserve what has proven value.
"What already exists that can be leveraged?"
03
Progress iteratively with feedback
Organize work into smaller pieces, deliver incrementally and use the feedback from each cycle to adjust direction before continuing.
"How do we validate before investing more?"
04
Collaborate and promote visibility
Work with the right people, remove information silos and increase transparency. Better decisions come from shared information.
"Who else needs to know this?"
05
Think and work holistically
No service, practice or process works in isolation. Understand the interdependencies and the impact of actions on the system as a whole.
"What is the impact beyond my domain?"
06
Keep it simple and practical
If a process, rule or action does not contribute to creating value, eliminate it. Unnecessary complexity is waste. Less is more when it works.
"Does this add value or is it bureaucracy?"
07
Optimize and automate
Maximize the value of human work by automating repetitive tasks. Optimize first — automating a bad process only multiplies the problem.
"What can be safely automated?"
⚖️
No principle operates alone
Apply them together, weighing each situation. They are guides, not rigid rules.
Service Value Chain (SVC) — 6 activities that transform demand into value
Atividade 1
Plan
Ensures shared understanding of the vision, current status and direction of improvement across all 4 levels: SVS, services, products and practices.
Questions it answers:
What is the organization's strategic direction?
Is the service portfolio aligned with the business?
How to allocate resources optimally?
Atividade 2
Engage
Ensures good understanding of stakeholder needs, transparency and continuous relationship with customers, users and partners.
Questions it answers:
Who are the stakeholders and what do they need?
How do we maintain communication and transparency?
How do we capture user feedback?
Atividade 3
Design & Transition
Ensures that new or changed services meet stakeholder expectations for quality, cost and time-to-market.
Questions it answers:
Does the design meet quality requirements?
Is the service ready to operate in production?
Have the transition risks been mitigated?
Atividade 4
Obtain / Build
Ensures that service components are available when and where needed, to agreed specifications — whether acquiring or building.
Questions it answers:
Build internally or hire/buy?
Do the components meet the requirements?
Are the assets catalogued and managed?
Atividade 5
Deliver & Support
Ensures service delivery as specified and user support. Manages incidents, requests and day-to-day operations.
Questions it answers:
Are services being delivered as promised?
Are incidents and requests resolved within SLA?
Are users satisfied?
Activity 6 — permeates everything
Improve
Ensures continual improvement in products, services and practices across all SVS levels. Uses the 7-step continual improvement model.
Questions it answers:
What is working and what needs improving?
Are we measuring what matters?
Are improvements generating real value?

🚨 Incident Management
Goal: restore service as quickly as possible, minimizing business impact
01
Identify & LogDetected by monitoring, user or automatically. Create ticket with symptom and impact.
02
Classify & PrioritizeUrgency × Impact = Priority. P1 critical → P4 low. Defines the resolution SLA.
03
Investigate & DiagnoseSeek initial root cause or workaround. Escalate to N2/N3 if needed.
04
Resolve & RestoreApply solution or workaround. Confirm with user that service has been restored.
05
Close & DocumentRecord solution, resolution time and satisfaction. Recurring incidents → open Problem.
📋 Service Request Management
Goal: handle predefined requests quickly, efficiently and in a standardized way
01
InitiateUser requests via portal, email or chat. Types: access, information, provisioning.
02
Approve (if required)High-impact or high-cost requests require approval. Automated approval workflow.
03
FulfillExecution by the responsible team with SLA defined in the service catalogue.
04
CloseConfirm delivery with the user. Measure satisfaction (CSAT). Update knowledge base.
Key difference: A Request is expected and predefined. An Incident is unexpected and disrupts the service.
🔍 Problem Management
Goal: reduce probability and impact of incidents by identifying and eliminating root causes
01
Identify ProblemRecurring incidents, trend alerts, proactive analysis. Open problem record.
02
Analyze Root Cause5 Whys, Fishbone, timeline analysis. Document Known Error when cause is identified.
03
Define SolutionWorkaround published in the knowledge base. Permanent fix via Change Management.
04
Resolver & RevisarImplement fix. Verify that associated incidents decreased. Update CMDB.
🎧 Service Desk
Single Point of Contact (SPOC) between IT and users. A channel, not just a tool.
N0
Self-service / AutomationPortal, chatbot, FAQs and knowledge base. Resolves without human interaction.
N1
First-Level SupportCentral team. Resolves ~70-80% of tickets. Triage, initial diagnosis and workarounds.
N2
Specialized SupportProduct or infrastructure technical teams. Resolves issues that N1 cannot resolve.
N3
Vendor / EngineeringVendor or product engineering. Bugs, patches and architecture issues.
Change Management — types and approval flow
Type 1
Standard Change
Pre-approved, low risk, documented procedure. Implemented without additional approval each time.
E.g.: Create user in AD, install approved software, routine security patch
Risk: Low · Approval: pre-approved
Type 2
Normal Change
Requires risk assessment and authorization by the CAB (Change Advisory Board) before implementation. Planned and scheduled.
E.g.: Server upgrade, new feature in production, database migration
Risk: Medium · Approval: CAB
Type 3
Emergency Change
Implemented as quickly as possible to resolve a critical incident. Accelerated process with ECAB (Emergency CAB) or delegated authority.
E.g.: Production hotfix, critical security patch, emergency rollback
Risk: High · Approval: ECAB/fast-track
CAB — Change Advisory Board
Group that evaluates and authorizes normal changes. Composition: Change Manager, technical representatives, business and relevant suppliers.
7Rs of Change
Raised · Reason · Return · Risks · Resources · Responsible · Relationship — mandatory questions in every change assessment.
Freeze periods
Periods when normal changes are blocked: Black Friday, fiscal close, major events. Only emergency changes are permitted.
Modelo de Continual Improvement — 7 passos
STEP 01
What is the vision?
Define the business vision, strategic objectives and context. What does the organization want to achieve? Connects improvement to strategy.
"What is the bigger goal?"
STEP 02
Where are we now?
Assess the current situation with objective data — not perceptions. Use metrics, benchmarks and current state mapping.
"What do the data show?"
STEP 03
Where do we want to be?
Define measurable and realistic targets. Use KPIs, OKRs or CSFs. The target must be specific enough to guide actions.
"What is the desired state?"
STEP 04
How do we get there?
Plan improvement actions with owners, timeline and resources. Prioritize by impact × effort. Iterate in short cycles.
"Which initiatives do we prioritize?"
STEP 05
Take action!
Implement the actions as planned. Monitor continuously during execution. Adjust in real time if needed.
"Are we executing as planned?"
STEP 06
Did we get there?
Measure the results and compare against the defined targets. Use the same indicators as step 2. Validate whether the improvement generated real value.
"Do the data confirm the improvement?"
STEP 07
Keep the momentum
Consolidate the gains, standardize the new state and use the learnings to start the next improvement cycle. Don't let progress regress.
"How do we sustain and improve further?"
SLA
Service Level Agreement
Agreement with the external customer. Defines the promised service level and penalties.
OLA
Operational Level Agreement
Internal agreement between IT teams. Supports the SLA with internal operational targets.
UC
Underpinning Contract
Contract with an external supplier that underpins the SLA delivered to the customer.
