The 3M — roots of waste in Lean
無駄
Muda — Waste
Any activity that consumes resources without creating value
It is the most visible of the 3M. Existem dois tipos: Type 1 (necessary but without direct value — regulatory, setup) and Type 2 (pure waste — eliminate immediately).
Exemplos de Muda:
Parts waiting in queue between processes
Unnecessary operator movement
Rework due to quality defects
Overproduction beyond demand
無理
Muri — Overburden
Excessive load on people, machines or processes
Occurs when more is demanded than natural capacity allows. Generates errors, breakdowns, burnout and accidents. The root cause of Muri is often an unresolved Mura.
Exemplos de Muri:
Employee at constant 120% allocation
Machine operating above rated capacity
Impossible deadlines that generate errors and rework
Unlimited WIP generating excessive multitasking
斑
Mura — Unevenness
Variation and inconsistency in the production flow
Variation in demand or process creates peaks and valleys. When there is a peak → Muri. When there is a valley → Muda. Mura is the link between the two and usually the most neglected cause.
Exemplos de Mura:
Demand concentrated at month end (push)
Large batches followed by idleness
Inconsistent quality between identical pieces
Unpredictable delivery speed to customer

01
Overproduction
Over-production
Producing more, sooner or faster than demand requires. It is the worst waste — it generates all others.
Signal: growing inventory without firm orders
Lean fix: sistema pull + Kanban
02
Waiting
Waiting
People, machines or materials idle waiting for the next process step.
Signal: operator idle waiting for machine or approval
Lean fix: balance line · heijunka
03
Transport
Transport
Unnecessary movement of materials or information between steps that add no value.
Signal: material traveling long distances in the layout
Lean fix: cellular layout · continuous flow
04
Over-processing
Over-processing
Doing more than necessary: extra steps, redundant approvals, reports nobody reads.
Signal: alignment meetings before the alignment
Lean fix: map VSM and eliminate steps
05
Inventory
Inventory
Accumulation of material, WIP or information beyond the minimum needed to maintain flow.
Signal: ticket queues, inflated backlog, stalled material
Lean fix: JIT · WIP limits
06
Motion
Motion
Unnecessary movement of people: fetching tools, clicking extra menus, switching systems.
Signal: operator walks a lot or switches between tabs/systems
Lean fix: 5S · workstation ergonomics
07
Defects
Defects
Any output that does not meet the required standard, generating rework, scrap or customer complaints.
Signal: rework rate above 0 · bugs in production
Lean fix: Poka-Yoke · source inspection
08
Unused talent
Skills (Non-utilized)
Not leveraging people's knowledge, creativity and skills. The 8th waste, added by modern Lean.
Signal: voiceless team, ignored suggestions, misallocated talents
Lean fix: Participatory Kaizen

Pareto Principle — the 80/20 rule
Defect A — late delivery40%40%
Defect B — quality rework25%65%
Defect C — communication failure15%80%
Defect D — missing inputs10%90%
Defect E — system error5%95%
Defect F — other (1)3%98%
Defect G — other (2)2%100%
80/20
80% dos problemas são causados por apenas 20% das causas. Foque onde o impacto é maior.
How to build a Pareto Chart
List all problems and their frequencies
Sort from highest to lowest
Calculate the cumulative % for each cause
Draw the bars + cumulative line
Mark the 80% point on the line — everything to the left is the action focus
Pareto Principle applications
Qualidade
20% of defect types cause 80% of customer complaints
Vendas
20% of customers generate 80% of revenue
Suporte
20% of bugs cause 80% of support tickets
Produção
20% of process steps cause 80% of lead time
Kaizen — continuous improvement tools
改善
Kaizen — Continuous Improvement
Culture of small daily improvements made by everyone. Not an event — it is a way of working. The sum of 1% improvement per day = 37× better in 1 year.
Princípio: nenhum problema é pequeno demais para melhorar
5S
5S — Workplace organization
Seiri (Sort) · Seiton (Set in order) · Seiso (Clean) · Seiketsu (Standardize) · Shitsuke (Sustain). Base de tudo.
Resultado: reduz Muda de movimento e defeitos por ambiente
現場
Gemba Walk — Go to the real place
Leaders go to the "shop floor" to observe real work. Problems are not visible in reports — they need to be seen at the Gemba (where work happens).
Ferramenta: observe · ask · respect the worker
5×なぜ
5 Porquês — Root cause
Ask "why?" 5 times consecutively about the same problem. Each answer becomes the next question. Reaches root cause without treating just the symptom.
Exemplo: Máquina parou → por quê? → fusível queimou → por quê? → sobrecarga → ...
ポカヨケ
Poka-Yoke — Error-proofing
Mechanisms that make it physically impossible to make a specific error. Eliminates defects at the source without relying on constant human attention.
Exemplos: USB só entra de um lado · validação automática de formulário
平準化
Heijunka — Leveling
Level workload over time to eliminate Mura. Distributes demand uniformly, avoiding peaks and valleys that generate Muri and Muda.
Lean fix: elimina o Mura que causa Muri e Muda
PDCA Cycle — scientific method for improvement
P
Plan
Identify the problem, analyze root cause (5 Whys / Ishikawa) and define an action plan with measurable target.
"What problem are we solving and how?"
D
Do
Implement the solution on a small scale (pilot). Collect data during execution. Document everything done and observed results.
"Did we do what we planned?"
C
Check
Compare results against the target defined in Plan. Use data, not opinion. Identify deviations and understand why they occurred.
"Do the results confirm the hypothesis?"
A
Act
If it worked: standardize and expand. If not: adjust the plan and restart the cycle. Cycle learnings feed the next Plan.
"Did we standardize or restart?"
↺  PDCA is iterative: the end of one cycle is the beginning of the next. Each loop generates learning that makes the next Plan more accurate. In the Toyota Way, PDCA runs at all levels — from operator to CEO.
